Planning Before Reasoning: A Vision-Language Dataset for Structured Stepwise Reasoning in Document Understanding

DocPlan: 133K document VQA examples annotated with query analysis, planning, extraction-and-reasoning, and answer stages

Abstract

This paper introduces DocPlan, a vision-language training dataset designed to emulate the stepwise reasoning strategies employed by human during visual document understanding. DocPlan was constructed to enable vision-language models (VLMs) to effectively learn the structured reasoning process that humans typically follow when interpreting documents. We reorganized existing document understanding datasets into 133K examples annotated with the following four sequential stages: Recognition → Planning → Extraction and Reasoning → Conclusion. To evaluate the effectiveness of DocPlan, we fine-tuned the direct prediction-based VLMs to perform stepwise reasoning generation across these stages. The experimental results demonstrated that our approach achieved up to a 13.8%p improvement in accuracy on document-based benchmarks.

Keywords Visual document understanding Document VQA Chart question answering Vision-language models (VLMs) Multimodal reasoning Stepwise reasoning Chain-of-thought (CoT) Plan-and-reason Synthetic reasoning data Interpretability

Dataset Construction

Existing document VQA datasets mostly pair an image with a short answer, so a model trained on them learns direct prediction and can reach the right answer through wrong intermediate reasoning (Figure 1 of the paper shows such a case on ChartQA). DocPlan follows the order in which people read charts and documents (recognize the question, plan, extract values, reason, conclude) and writes it out as explicit tagged stages. It is built as follows:

  1. Image collection. All training images of DocVQA, InfoVQA, and ChartQA are reused unchanged (about 28K images; 28,954 in the final dataset).
  2. Query generation. Because original queries are mostly simple extraction questions, Qwen2.5-VL generates 80K new queries of five document-specific types adapted from MMStar (Fine-grained Perception, Coarse Perception, Instance Reasoning, Logical Reasoning, Mathematics) at three difficulty levels (Basic, Intermediate, Advanced). With the 82K original queries this gives 162K queries.
  3. Plan-and-reason generation. For every query, Qwen2.5-VL writes a query analysis, then a plan with N sub-plans (N chosen by query type and difficulty), then executes each sub-plan in turn by extracting the needed information and reasoning over it, reusing earlier intermediate answers, and finally writes the final answer.
  4. Filtering. Original queries (with ground truth) pass a rule-based tag check, then GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-VL, and Gemini 2.0 rate each sample and only samples on which the models agree are kept. Generated queries (without ground truth) are first checked by GPT-4o for validity against the image, then pass the same tag check and model-agreement filtering.

The result is DocPlan with 133,623 samples (68,513 from original queries and 65,110 from generated queries). An extra trace-based “hard filtering” step with human review of 10,207 likely errors produces DocPlan-HF with 124,053 samples. Both are released on Hugging Face (DocPlan-133K, DocPlan-HF-124K; access is granted on request). VLMs are fine-tuned on the data with visual instruction tuning for three epochs and evaluated with VLMEvalKit on DocVQA and InfoVQA (ANLS) and ChartQA and ChartQAPro (GPT-4o as judge).

Main Results

  • Up to 13.8%p higher average accuracy. Training paligemma2-10B on DocPlan raises its average over DocVQA, InfoVQA, ChartQA, and ChartQAPro from 40.1 to 53.9; Llama3.2-11B-Vision goes from 52.3 to 69.2 (59.3 when trained on the original QA data without reasoning).
  • Planning beats direct prediction on the same data. Rewriting the original 82K QA pairs into the DocPlan format (+DocPlan(origin)) improves Llama3.2 by 7.1%p (59.3 → 66.4) and gemma3 by 2.4%p (58.6 → 61.0) over direct training; adding the generated queries gives Llama3.2 another 2.8%p (69.2).
  • Structured stages beat free-form chain-of-thought. With the same base models, DocPlan outperforms free-form CoT, e.g. Gemma3-4b DocVQA 82.8 vs. 53.46 and ChartQA 81.3 vs. 54.0, and Llama-Vision InfoVQA 71.2 vs. 54.83. In the stage ablation, adding the execution stage gives the largest gain (5.3%p).
  • Competitive with larger systems and transfers to held-out data. Llama-3.2-11B+DocPlan reaches 91.5 on DocVQA and 86.6 on ChartQA, above Llava-CoT (89.8 / 82.6) and zero-shot GPT-4o (85.6 / 85.7). On held-out TextVQA, Gemma3-4B-it improves from 58.14 to 62.91 (+4.77%p).
  • More interpretable reasoning traces. In a simulatability test (answering from the reasoning trace alone, without the image), Llama3.2+DocPlan scores 62.0 on average vs. 51.5 for Llava-CoT (+10.5%p); in GPT pairwise preference it wins 71.3% of comparisons on average vs. 26.1% for Llava-CoT.

Trade-offs reported in the paper: responses become much longer (e.g. Llama3.2-11B-V generates 177.5–285.3 tokens on average instead of 5.7–26.1), and accuracy on the general-domain MMMU benchmark drops after document-only fine-tuning, though less than with direct-prediction training (Llama3.2-11B-Vision: −15.0%p vs. −31.2%p).
